SEC-02 The most dangerous number

From session b3
model accuracy 96.8% always answers "no" 96.8% buyers actually caught 0

The two models score the same. One of them is not a model.

The most dangerous number in ML is a high accuracy.
What to ask for instead

ROC-AUC tells the honest story here, and precision and recall arrive in b7. A single headline score cannot distinguish a model from a shrug.

SEC-03 Which metric tells the truth

b7

Session b8 judges a regression in dollars, because a metric nobody can price is a metric nobody acts on.
Pick the metric before you train

Choosing the score after seeing the results is how a mediocre model gets promoted. Decide what good means while the question is still about the business, not about the run you just did.

SEC-06 Four ways a great score lies

LeakageSomething from the future, or from the answer, is in the features. Ask: could this column exist at prediction time?
An imbalanced classAlways answering no scores 96.8 percent and catches nobody. Ask: what does the trivial model score?
Tuning on the test setEvery peek spends a little of the only honest estimate you have. Ask: how many times has this set been looked at?
OverfittingThe model learned the sample rather than the pattern. Ask: how far apart are train and validation?
Sessions b2 and b9 exist because leakage is arrangement, not carelessness. A Pipeline makes it structurally impossible rather than merely unlikely.
The common thread

None of these produce an error. Every one of them produces a number that looks better than the truth, which is why the leader track spends a whole session on reading a report card rather than on how models work.

SEC-07 The one picture

Session b4 calls it the single most important picture in machine learning, and the rest of the builder track is a response to it.
under
too simpleWrong on the training data and wrong on new data. The model has not learned enough.
right
the middleWhere training and validation error are both low and close together.
over
too complexRight on the training data, wrong on new data. The model memorised the sample.
Bias-variance is the dial. Regularization, cross-validation and honest scoring are how you find where to set it.
